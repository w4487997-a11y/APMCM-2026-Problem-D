\section{Data and Audit}
\label{sec:data-audit}

\subsection{Official data universe and file roles}

The official data form a complete $52\times52\times7$ factorial grid with
18,928 ordered compositions and no missing combination cells
\cite{apmcm2026problemD}.  Each row has a stable numerical identifier and the
composition fields \texttt{formula}, A, B, and X.  The two labeled files add
structural and electronic quantities, the five penalty components, and the
total $V$.  The remaining pools expose composition only.  Table~\ref{tab:dataset-roles}
records the roles used in this study.

Files~02 and~03 contain 15,225 labeled rows in total, while file~04 contains
the remaining 3,703 compositions.  Equivalently, the official split assigns
1,608, 567, and 529 complete ordered $(A,B)$ groups to files~02, 03, and~04;
each group contains all seven X values.  These counts sum to all 2,704
ordered cation pairs.  Pool~07 contains each pair once at NO2, and pool~08
contains every pair at O3, NO2, and FO2.  Pools~11 and~12 are the intersections
of these candidate domains with the 529 blind cation pairs; they are not
additional labeled data.

\subsection{Schema, target reconstruction, and integrity checks}

All source CSV files were read as UTF-8 with optional byte-order marks.  The
composition key is the ordered triple $(A,B,X)$, and \texttt{id} ranges from
0 to 18,927 over the full grid.  The audit preserved source row order and
formula text, verified uniqueness at the appropriate pool level, and kept
leading zeros in two-character pool codes.  No row was deleted, imputed, or
augmented with an external materials database.

The nine official SHA-256 checks all matched.  The eight CSV schemas, row
counts, key relationships, and candidate-pool intersections also matched the
data contract.  Physical and penalty columns in the labeled files were
finite and nonnegative where required; zero band gap was retained as the
official metallic state rather than treated as missing.  Table~\ref{tab:label-audit}
summarizes the principal independent checks.

The small reconstruction differences are consistent with the six-decimal
storage precision.  They do not justify changing Equation~\eqref{eq:formulation-V}
or expanding the strict feasible region.  The problem statement reports 19
zero-violation records and 15 strictly feasible records in the complete
dataset.  Because neither public labeled split contains such a record, their
identities belong to the hidden part of the data and were not inferred.

\subsection{Zero-gap dominance and low-tail scarcity}

The response distribution is strongly asymmetric.  Table~\ref{tab:data-tail}
shows that approximately 96\% of each labeled split has $E_g=0$.  By
Equation~\eqref{eq:formulation-hinge}, a zero-gap record incurs a lower-gap
penalty of $\phi(1.4)=7.84$ before any other violation is counted.  At the
same time, only 222 training rows and 70 validation rows have $V<5$.

Within the observed $V<5$ subsets, formation-energy and CBM penalties account
for 80.7\% of the summed penalty in file~02 and 77.5\% in file~03.  This does
not imply causality, but it shows why global accuracy alone is an incomplete
screening criterion.  A model can fit the majority metallic regime yet rank
the small low-$V$ tail poorly.  It also motivates the separate zero/positive
gap treatment evaluated in Section~\ref{sec:q1-surrogate}.

\subsection{Training protocols for Q1 and Q2/Q3}

The Q1 development protocol keeps every ordered $(A,B)$ pair intact.  The
1,608 file~02 groups were deterministically assigned to five folds using seed
42, with 321 or 322 groups per validation fold.  All seven X variants of a
pair therefore remain on the same side of a fold boundary.  Preprocessors and
categorical encoders are fitted anew inside each training fold.  After the
candidate specifications are frozen, file~03 is used once for formal model
selection.  The released \texttt{q1\_final} fit uses file~02 only, and its
file~04 output is a blind prediction table rather than a labeled test result.

Q2 and Q3 require candidate scores rather than an independent estimate of Q1
generalization.  Their \texttt{q23\_final} fit therefore reuses the frozen Q1
architecture but is trained on files~02 and~03 together, as prescribed by the
problem.  It scores files~07, 08, 11, and~12 without reading physical labels.
The resulting 07/08 scores feed the full-pool QUBO models.  This refit is not
used to revise the Q1 validation metrics, and the 03 rows are never presented
as unseen evidence after entering the refit.

The current integrated Q2/Q3 results concern the complete 07 and 08 pools.
Although 11 and 12 are composition subsets of those domains, selecting their
rows after a full-pool solve would not be an independent blind-pool
optimization.  Until dedicated matrices and runs exist for these two files,
the manuscript treats that comparison as pending.

\subsection{Leakage controls and evidence boundary}

The following controls are applied throughout the workflow.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The predictor whitelist is A, B, and X, plus only deterministic
  composition-derived features defined within a model variant.  Identifiers,
  row positions, pool labels, and physical targets are excluded.
  \item Ordered-pair grouping is used for all internal folds, preventing the
  seven anion variants of one cation pair from crossing a train--validation
  boundary.
  \item Feature encoders are fitted within each training fold.  Validation
  labels never determine categories, transformations, or hyperparameters
  after the selection rule is frozen.
  \item Q1 and Q2/Q3 use separate fitted model artifacts.  Predictions from
  the 02+03 refit cannot be substituted for Q1's file~03 evidence.
  \item Candidate files are scored in their original order, ties are resolved
  by numerical identifier, and model, input, and output hashes are recorded.
  \item File~04 truth and organizer scoring are not available locally.  No
  external lookup, material-name matching, or reverse engineering is used to
  infer hidden labels.
\end{enumerate}

These controls establish a traceable boundary between model development,
blind inference, and combinatorial optimization.  They do not eliminate
surrogate error; instead, they make its source and its downstream role
explicit.
